FlagFort playtest review - seed 1ugknrj-necr1t

A winning fort that never felt safe

This single normal campaign shows strong automated defense and zero flag pressure, but extreme structure attrition. The main balancing risk is not simply that the run was too easy or too hard. It is that the adaptive system reads investment as permission to amplify attrition while its feedback correction cannot lower difficulty.

Highest-value findings

High confidence - configuration

The correction system cannot relieve pressure

The pressure penalty reaches its maximum of 1 on night 3 and stays there except for 0.9613 on night 4. Yet performanceDifficultyDelta() clamps the result to a non-negative value, so difficult outcomes can only cancel an upward bonus. They can never produce a downward correction.

  • Correction is +0.1044 after night 1 and +0.0941 after night 2.
  • Correction is exactly 0 after every later night, despite 26 additional structures being destroyed.
Recommendation: Decide whether this is intentionally an upward-only anti-snowball system. If it is meant to adapt both ways, add a bounded negative delta and test its recovery behavior. If it is intentionally upward-only, rename it and do not rely on it as a pressure safety valve.
High confidence - configuration

Night 10 stacks three difficulty spikes

Expected fort points drop from 3,185 on night 9 to 2,030 on night 10. The actual fort rises from 3,491 to 3,786, so the structure modifier jumps from 1.0332 to its 1.5 cap. With the level-19 delta, the effective multiplier jumps from 1.3032 to 1.77.

  • Requested scheduled threat rises from 60 to 94, a 57% increase.
  • The boss is outside that roster and has threat 40.
  • The boss plus five summons explain 73 spawned versus 67 scheduled.
  • Night 10 incoming damage reaches 2,306, the run maximum.
Recommendation: Treat night 10 as one budget. Either restore a monotonic expected-fort curve, or explicitly subtract boss threat and summon liability from scheduled threat. Test each version rather than tuning the base wave in isolation.
Medium confidence - balance

Fortification may be converting into replacement chores

The fort grows far above expectation while losing 27 structures worth 1,202 points and taking damage across structures worth 5,455 points. That suggests a potentially successful but labor-heavy loop: build more, trigger a stronger horde, lose more, rebuild.

  • Actual fort value climbs from 282 on night 2 to 3,786 on night 10.
  • Incoming damage accelerates much faster than breaches: the flag takes no damage after three enemies enter its radius on night 1.
  • Nights 7, 8, and 10 account for 5,139 damage, 58% of the run total.
Recommendation: Do not lower all structure damage yet. First log repair and rebuild time, resources spent replacing losses, and structure lifespan. Balance for meaningful breaches without making successful fort design feel taxed merely for existing.
Confirmed by player - combat balance

Four diamond turrets fully automated the finale

The player confirmed doing almost no damage on night 10 while a base with four diamond turrets cleared essentially the whole wave and boss. This is stronger evidence than kill share alone: the final encounter did not require active player combat.

  • A diamond turret deals 30 base damage every 0.62 seconds, or 48.4 base DPS.
  • Four provide about 193.5 base DPS before temporary or permanent turret upgrades.
  • The night-10 adaptive multiplier gives the boss about 1,348 health before challenges. Four unupgraded diamond turrets can theoretically remove that in about 7 seconds if all shots connect.
  • Personal kill share falls to 0% on night 10, while all 73 enemies die and the flag takes no damage.
Recommendation: First decide the desired payoff. If four top-tier turrets are meant to be a winning capstone, preserve their power and focus on spectacle, telemetry, and making the boss threaten something before dying. If the boss should require player participation, target a 12 to 18 second survival window against four diamond turrets and test a boss-specific interaction. Avoid a broad turret nerf that would weaken the earned build across every earlier night.

Telemetry to add before broad numeric tuning

The current log is excellent for wave and adaptive math, but it cannot tell whether the player had fun, felt busy, or paid an excessive rebuild tax. Add final and per-night structure inventory by kind and tier, structures built/upgraded/repaired/destroyed, resources gathered/spent/unspent, damage and kills by source, enemy damage by kind and target, boss kill time, cards chosen, permanent upgrades/equipment, and time spent gathering/building/repairing/fighting. Also separate scheduled enemies, boss, summons, and children in the executive summary.

What not to conclude from this run

Do not globally nerf enemy damage yet

High attrition may be concentrated in popper bursts, rammer charges, boss attacks, or fort geometry. The log does not attribute damage by enemy or source.

Do not globally nerf turrets yet

Four diamond turrets are a large endgame investment and should feel powerful. Determine whether the design goal is an earned automated victory or mandatory player participation, then tune the boss encounter specifically.

Do not generalize across profiles

Level 19 adds +0.27 to every adaptive multiplier. Run fresh-profile and max-progression controls before changing the level curve.
