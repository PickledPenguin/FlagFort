FlagFort interface experiment

Keep the clock.
Clear the battlefield.

Five HUD directions that preserve FlagFort’s chunky, raised forest-fort style while making the central countdown non-blocking, reducing accidental actions, and giving the palette more range.

The decision
  • Choose a primary visual direction, or mix pieces from several.
  • Every clock is view-only, fades near the pointer, and never captures a build click.
  • Every skip-night action moves outside the central world target zone.

Current 960 × 540 gameplay capture

What we are improving

The current HUD already has strong identity. The issue is spatial, not foundational: a tall opaque clock and its button overlap the flag’s most useful building area.

KeepRaised panels, rounded heavy borders, Impact numerals, restrained iconography, forest green foundation.
Fix174 × 138 px opaque clock footprint plus a clickable skip action in the center build corridor.
RefineSeparate information from action. The timer remains central; ending the day becomes a deliberate peripheral command.
◉
Shared interaction rule for every direction

The clock uses pointer-events: none. Global pointer tracking lowers it to roughly 12 to 18% opacity when the cursor enters a generous proximity radius. World clicks still reach building, repair, attacks, and selection behind it. Keyboard and gamepad users retain a permanently readable clock because no pointer proximity is active.

Clock

Smallest footprint and highest contrast. No decorative circle because the number itself is the content.

Skip placement

Top-right under resources in a labeled “Day controls” group. Location is predictable and outside play space.

Safety

Use a short hold, double-confirm, or existing confirmation modal. Never bind skip-night to an unmodified hotkey.

Density

8 px spacing rhythm, shared 12 px corners, fewer nested outlines, consistent left-to-right reading.

Tradeoff: The strict restraint improves readability but loses some of the current oversized toy-like charm.
